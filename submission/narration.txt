A machine alarm tells an engineer that something changed. It rarely tells them why, which procedure applies, or what evidence to bring to the repair. While they search manuals and call the site, a small fault can become a long interruption.

Kairos AI is an industrial reliability copilot. It learns from each device's own documents, then connects that knowledge to telemetry. An engineer can ask a question, plan a task, or let Agent mode prepare the next step.

Live monitoring is the headline: watch device parameters stream into the same analysis engine used for historical data. This demo uses generated readings. In production, an authorized device would connect through an approved REST endpoint and secure network path. Monitoring is read-only.

A manual can go stale. Tavily searches public manufacturer guidance, maps documentation links, and extracts evidence. Nemotron builds a cited brief for engineer review. We show exactly what was found—and what remains unknown—without sending private device records to web search.

Here is a voltage-sag example. Monitor detects an abnormal change against the device's learned healthy baseline. Diagnose sends the evidence and relevant procedure to NVIDIA Nemotron through Nebius Token Factory; the app checks the structured answer and verifies its procedure citations. Decide is a separate deterministic safety gate: critical or uncertain cases go to human review. Act prepares a review-only work order or a human-approved sandbox scenario—never a real equipment command. Verify checks later readings and reports whether the simulated condition recovered or must remain escalated.

The reasoning call is made at runtime through Nebius Token Factory using Nemotron-3.5-Lightning. We retain Cloud Run and optional Google Cloud persistence, but the diagnosis path shown here is Nebius—not a screenshot or a silent fallback. Any downtime or cost avoided is an assumption-based estimate, not measured savings.

Kairos AI turns scattered alarms into evidence an engineer can act on. Better context, faster triage, and people still in control.
